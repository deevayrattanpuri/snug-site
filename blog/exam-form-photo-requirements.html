Exam and government form photos: reading the requirements properly

These forms are the strictest you will meet, and the least forgiving about a resubmission. Get it right the first time.

Presets 14 July 2026 ·6 min read

Exam boards and government portals specify photographs in more detail than almost anyone else, and their upload validators are unusually literal. A photo that is 1 KB over the limit is rejected as firmly as one that is 10 MB over.

Write down every number before you start

Requirements are typically scattered across a notice, a help page and sometimes a PDF. Collect them all first:

  • File size — usually both a maximum and, often overlooked, a minimum
  • Dimensions — in pixels, or in millimetres with a required resolution
  • Format — almost always JPG
  • Background — typically white or light grey, plain
  • Recency — often within the last three or six months
  • Signature — frequently a separate upload with its own rules

The minimum file size catches people out constantly. Compress a photo to 15 KB to be safe and a form demanding at least 20 KB will refuse it.

The signature is a separate problem

Many forms want a scanned signature alongside the photo, with its own dimensions and size limit. Sign in black or dark blue ink on plain white paper, photograph it in even light, crop tightly to the signature, and resize to the stated dimensions. If the background comes out grey, the usual cause is shadow — move to a brighter spot rather than trying to fix it afterwards.

Getting under a tight limit

Exam portals often want under 50 KB, sometimes under 20 KB. That is severe, and the order of operations matters more than ever:

  1. Crop to the required ratio, tightly — head and shoulders only.
  2. Resize to the exact pixel dimensions specified, and no larger.
  3. Save as JPG.
  4. Only now set the target file size.

A tightly cropped portrait at the exact required dimensions has very little to describe, so it reaches a small file size while still looking clean.

Keep the files you submit

Save the exact image you uploaded, named clearly, somewhere you will find it. Portals lose uploads, applications get queried, and the same photo is often needed again for the admit card or the interview stage. Reproducing it from memory months later is miserable.

Submit early

Portals get slow and unreliable near a deadline, and that is exactly when a rejected upload becomes a crisis. Doing it a week early turns a rejection into an inconvenience.
