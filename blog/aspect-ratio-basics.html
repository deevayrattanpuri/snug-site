Aspect ratio: why your photo keeps getting cropped badly

Platforms crop to their own shape whether you like it or not. Choosing the crop yourself is the whole trick.

Sizing 4 August 2026 ·5 min read

You upload a perfectly good photo and the site cuts someone's head off. Nothing was wrong with the picture — it was the wrong shape, and something decided where to cut.

Ratio, not size

Aspect ratio is the relationship between width and height, independent of how big the image is. 4000 × 3000 and 800 × 600 are both 4:3. One is twelve megapixels and one is less than half a megapixel, and they are exactly the same shape.

The ratios you meet

RatioShapeWhere it turns up
1:1SquareProfile pictures, many marketplace thumbnails
4:3Slightly wideThe default on most phone cameras
3:2WiderStandard 35 mm photography, 4 × 6 prints
16:9WidescreenVideo, banners, cover images
9:16TallStories, reels, vertical video
35:45Slightly tallPassport and ID photos in much of the world

The three ways to change shape

Crop cuts pixels off the edges. The remaining picture keeps its proportions and nothing distorts — you simply lose whatever was at the edges. This is nearly always the right answer.

Stretch keeps everything and squashes it into the new shape. Faces go wide or narrow, circles become ovals, and it looks wrong even to people who cannot say why. Avoid it.

Fit with padding keeps the whole image and adds bars at the sides to make up the shape. Nothing is lost and nothing distorts, at the cost of visible borders. This is the right call when a form demands a square and the photo genuinely cannot be cropped.

Crop deliberately, or the platform will

Upload a 4:3 photo where a square is expected and something has to go. Automatic cropping usually takes the centre, which is how heads end up removed and horizons end up in odd places.

Cropping it yourself first means you decide what survives. It takes ten seconds and it is the difference between a portrait and an accident.

Shoot a little loose

If you know a photo is destined for several places with different shapes, leave breathing room. A subject framed tightly in 4:3 cannot be cropped to 16:9 without losing the top of their head, and cannot become 9:16 at all.

A small amount of extra space around the subject is what makes one photo work everywhere.

The short version

Crop to the required ratio yourself. Automatic cropping takes the centre, and the centre is rarely the point.
