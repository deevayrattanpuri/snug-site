HEIC to JPG: why your phone photos won't upload

Your photo opens perfectly on your phone and is rejected everywhere else. The format is why.

Formats 29 September 2026 ·5 min read

You pick a photo from your gallery, the upload form thinks about it, and then refuses. The file looked completely normal a second ago. What changed is that something other than your phone tried to read it.

What HEIC actually is

HEIC (sometimes HEIF) is a modern image container built on the HEVC video codec. It is genuinely better than JPEG at the thing it was designed for: it stores roughly the same visual quality in about half the bytes, supports proper transparency, handles wider colour, and can hold several images in one file — which is how burst shots and depth effects work.

Apple made it the iPhone default in 2017 and many Android cameras now offer it too. For storing photos on the device it is an excellent choice. The trouble starts when the file leaves.

Why so much software still can't read it

JPEG is thirty-plus years old and support for it is simply assumed. HEIC is newer, and the codec underneath it carries patent licensing that made a lot of software vendors cautious. The result is patchy support: your phone and your laptop are fine, while the government portal built in 2015 and the recruitment system your employer licenses are not.

Those are exactly the systems you most need to upload to.

How to tell what you have

Look at the file extension when you share or export the photo — .heic or .heif rather than .jpg. On many phones the gallery never shows you the extension at all, which is why this catches people repeatedly.

Two ways to fix it

Convert the photo you need. Open it in a converter, choose JPG, save. You keep the space savings on everything else in your library and only convert what has to travel.

Change the camera setting. On iPhone, Settings › Camera › Formats › Most Compatible. On Android it varies by manufacturer, usually under camera settings as a format or storage option. Your photos take more space from then on, but they work everywhere.

The first option is usually better. HEIC is saving you real storage, and the number of photos you actually upload to strict systems is small.

Does converting lose quality?

A little, unavoidably. Going from HEIC to JPG is a re-encode, and JPEG is lossy, so the conversion discards some information. In practice, at a reasonable quality setting, you will not see it — especially after the form compresses your upload anyway.

What matters more is not converting repeatedly. Convert once from the original, not from a JPG you already converted and re-saved twice.

Doing it in Snug

Open the HEIC photo, choose JPG on the format row, and save. The converted file is written as a new image in Pictures/Snug, so the HEIC original stays in your gallery exactly as it was. If the form also has a size limit, set that at the same time and do both in one pass.

The short version

HEIC is better at storage and worse at travelling. Keep it on the phone, convert to JPG for anything you upload.
