Passport photo sizes: the measurements that actually matter

Most people get the outer dimensions right and the head height wrong. That is the one that gets rejected.

Presets 22 September 2026 ·7 min read

Passport photo rules look like they are about the size of the picture. They are mostly about the size of your head inside it.

The two common formats

Almost every country uses one of two shapes:

FormatPhysical sizeAt 300 DPIUsed by
35 × 45 mm3.5 × 4.5 cm413 × 531 px UK, EU, India, Australia and many others
2 × 2 inch51 × 51 mm600 × 600 px United States, and several countries following its standard

Always confirm against the authority you are applying to. These are the common cases, not a universal rule, and requirements do change.

The measurement people miss

Within that frame, the rules specify how much of it your head should occupy — typically measured from chin to the crown of the head, and often also where your eyes should sit vertically.

For the 35 × 45 mm format, head height is commonly required to fall between 29 mm and 34 mm. For the US 2 × 2 inch format it is usually 1 inch to 1 3/8 inches, with the eyes between 1 1/8 and 1 3/8 inches from the bottom.

This is where most rejections come from. People photograph themselves at arm's length, crop to the right outer dimensions, and end up with a head that fills far too little of the frame — or, taking a selfie too close, far too much.

Resolution, and why 300 DPI keeps appearing

If the photo will be printed, it needs enough pixels to survive printing. 300 DPI is the long-standing standard for photographic print quality, which is how 35 × 45 mm becomes 413 × 531 pixels.

Submitting a digital photo at lower resolution risks a soft print. Submitting a vastly higher one is usually harmless but may breach a file size limit.

The rules a resizer cannot help with

Getting the geometry right gets you past the automated checks. A human, or an automated content check, still looks at:

  • Background — plain, light, uniform, no shadows cast on it
  • Expression — neutral, mouth closed, both eyes open and visible
  • Lighting — even, no harsh shadows across the face, no flash glare
  • Glasses — increasingly not allowed at all; where permitted, no reflections and no frames obscuring the eyes
  • Head coverings — generally only for religious reasons, and the full face must remain visible
  • Recency — usually taken within the last six months

A practical approach

  1. Stand about two metres from a plain, light wall, with the camera at eye level.
  2. Have someone else take it, on the rear camera, from roughly 1.5 to 2 metres away. Selfie distance distorts facial proportions noticeably.
  3. Use soft, even light — a window to the side, not a direct flash.
  4. Crop to the required ratio, positioning the head to hit the chin-to-crown measurement.
  5. Resize to the required pixel dimensions and bring it under the file size ceiling.
