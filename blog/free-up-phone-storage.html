Free up phone storage without deleting a single memory

"Storage almost full" does not have to mean choosing which photos to lose. A handful of files are usually doing most of the damage.

Storage 25 August 2026 ·6 min read

The notification always arrives at the worst possible moment — usually as you are trying to take the photo. And the standard advice is to delete things, which means sitting down and deciding which parts of your life are expendable.

There is almost always a better option, because photo libraries are extraordinarily lopsided.

A small number of files, most of the space

Scroll through any phone's storage breakdown and the pattern repeats: a few dozen photos account for a disproportionate share of the gigabytes. Burst sequences, panoramas, screenshots of screenshots, photos of documents taken at full resolution, and anything shot in a high-quality mode.

Forty photos using 1.6 GB is not unusual. Resize those forty and you might recover well over a gigabyte while keeping every single picture.

Resolution you cannot actually see

A 12-megapixel photo is 4000 pixels across. Your phone screen is perhaps 1200 pixels across. When you look at a photo on your own phone, you are seeing roughly a quarter of the detail that is stored.

That extra resolution matters for printing, for serious cropping, and for archiving. For the hundreds of photos you will only ever scroll past, it is paying rent and contributing nothing.

What to resize and what to leave

  • Resize freely: photos of documents, receipts, whiteboards, parking spaces, screenshots, anything you took as a note rather than a picture.
  • Resize carefully: everyday snapshots you want to keep but will never print. 2000 px on the long edge still looks excellent on any phone.
  • Leave alone: anything you might print or enlarge, anything of genuine personal importance, and anything you may want to crop heavily later.

The safe way to do it

The nervous part is obvious: you are shrinking photos you want to keep. The protection is to never overwrite the original in the same step.

Snug writes every result as a new file in Pictures/Snug. You resize, you look at the results, and only then do you delete the originals — deliberately, having seen what you are replacing them with. Replacing an original in place is possible but it is a separate choice that asks for confirmation first.

A workable routine

  1. Find the largest photos in your library rather than guessing.
  2. Separate out anything irreplaceable and leave it alone.
  3. Batch resize the rest to something sensible — 2000 px on the long edge is a good default.
  4. Check a handful of the results properly.
  5. Only now delete the originals you replaced.

Do this before you buy more storage

Paying monthly for cloud storage to hold photos at four times the resolution you will ever look at is a strange trade. Run the exercise once, see how much comes back, and then decide whether you actually need the subscription.

The short version

Find the biggest files, resize those, check the results, then delete the originals. Keep every photo, lose the gigabytes.
