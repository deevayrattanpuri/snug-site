Turning photos into a PDF that an application will accept

One file, pages in order, small enough to attach. Most rejected documents fail on the last of those.

Workflow 28 July 2026 ·5 min read

Applications ask for PDFs because a PDF is one file with the pages in a fixed order. Fifteen loose photos named IMG_4471 are a filing problem for whoever receives them.

Get the photos right first

A PDF built from bad photos is a bad PDF. Before combining anything:

  • Crop to the page. Cut away the table, your hand and the surrounding floor. This alone often halves the file size.
  • Straighten. A document shot at an angle looks careless and is harder to read.
  • Check legibility. Every number and signature must be readable at 100%. If you cannot read it, neither can the person assessing your application.
  • Resize sensibly. A full 12-megapixel photo of an A4 page is far more than anyone needs. Around 1500–2000 px on the long edge keeps text crisp.

Order is part of the document

Put the pages in the order they would appear on paper. If the application lists required documents in a specific sequence, follow it exactly. Whoever opens the file is working through a checklist, and making them hunt is an avoidable way to irritate them.

The size limit is where most attempts fail

Portals commonly cap uploads at 2 MB, sometimes 1 MB. Fifteen unresized phone photos will produce a PDF of 40 MB or more, and no amount of trying again will get it through.

Because the pages come from images, the PDF is as big as the images you put in. Shrink them first and the document comes out small enough to attach without having to compress the finished PDF, which usually looks worse.

A rough budget

PagesTarget per pageFinished PDF
1–3400–600 KBComfortably under 2 MB
4–8200–300 KBAround 1–2 MB
9–15100–150 KBAround 1–2 MB

Text survives compression better than photographs do, so document scans can go lower than you might expect before legibility suffers.

Name it like a human

surname-passport-and-proof-of-address.pdf is better than document(3).pdf in every possible way, including for you when you need to find it again in six months.

Check it before sending

Open the finished PDF and read it. Every page present, right way up, in order, legible. It takes thirty seconds and catches the upside-down page that would otherwise cost you a week.

The short version

Crop and resize the photos before combining. A PDF is only as small and as legible as the images inside it.
