Marketplace photos that actually sell the thing

Buyers scroll fast and judge in a second. Size and clarity do more work than you would think.

Presets 30 June 2026 ·5 min read

On a classifieds or marketplace app, your photo competes against a grid of other photos at thumbnail size. It has about a second to look like something worth tapping.

The first photo is the whole decision

Everything after the first image is for people already interested. The first one has one job: show the entire item, clearly, filling the frame, against a background that is not distracting.

Shoot it in daylight, near a window, with the item on a plain surface. No flash — it flattens everything and produces hard shadows. Fill the frame so the item is not a small object in a large room.

Size it for a grid, not a gallery

Most marketplaces display square thumbnails. If your photo is a wide landscape shot, the grid will crop it to a square and you do not control where. Crop to 1:1 yourself and keep the item centred with a little margin.

For resolution, around 1200–1600 px square is comfortable: sharp when tapped, quick to load on a patchy connection. Marketplace apps recompress uploads heavily, so an enormous file gains you nothing.

Photograph the flaws deliberately

This feels counterintuitive and it is the single best thing you can do. A clear photo of the scuff, the chip, the worn corner does three things: it filters out buyers who would have complained, it makes every other claim in your listing more believable, and it heads off the "you didn't mention this" message after collection.

A useful set of shots

  1. The whole item, square, well lit — this is your thumbnail
  2. The other side or the back
  3. Any identifying detail: label, model number, serial
  4. Close-ups of every flaw worth mentioning
  5. Something for scale, if the size is not obvious
  6. Accessories, boxes or manuals included in the sale

Strip the location data

This is the part people forget. Photos taken at home carry the GPS coordinates of your home, and marketplace listings do not always strip metadata on upload. You are posting an item for sale to the public; you are not posting your address.

Removing metadata takes one step and removes the problem entirely. Snug's Metadata tool writes a clean copy and leaves your original, with its date and location intact, in your own library.

Batch the whole listing

Six photos of one item want identical treatment, so run them through together: same crop ratio, same dimensions, same size limit, metadata stripped. A consistent set looks considered, and a considered listing sells faster than an apologetic one.

The short version

Square crop, daylight, fill the frame, photograph the flaws — and strip the location data before you post.
