Watermarks: useful, or just in the way?

A watermark is a trade between protection and legibility. Most people get the balance wrong in the same direction.

Workflow 28 April 2026 ·4 min read

Watermarks exist because publishing an image puts it beyond your control. Whether one is worth adding depends entirely on what the photo is for.

When a watermark earns its place

  • Marketplace listings — your photos get copied into fake listings surprisingly often. A mark makes that immediately obvious to buyers.
  • Work shown to a client before payment — proofs and previews.
  • Portfolio images on sites that make right-click saving trivial.
  • Anything you expect to be reposted, where attribution matters to you.

When it is just damage

  • Photos for friends and family
  • Anything submitted to a form or application — it will be rejected
  • Photos where the subject detail is the point and the mark covers it
  • Images you are posting to a platform that strips or crops them anyway

The design rules

A good watermark is noticed once and then ignored. A bad one is all anyone sees.

  • Corner, not centre. Centre marks ruin the photo for the honest viewer and are not much harder to remove.
  • Low opacity. Around 30–50% is readable without competing with the image.
  • Small. A tenth of the image width is plenty.
  • Never across a face, or the detail a buyer is trying to inspect.
  • Consistent. The same mark in the same place across a listing looks professional; a different one each time looks chaotic.

Be realistic about protection

A watermark is a deterrent, not a lock. Anyone determined can crop it out or paint over it. What it actually does is raise the effort above the threshold of casual copying, and make an unauthorised use obvious to a third party.

That is worth having. It is just not the same as security.

Keep a clean original

This matters more than the watermark itself. The moment you need a version without the mark — a sale, a print, a client who has now paid — you need the unmarked file.

Snug applies the watermark to the copy it saves and leaves the original untouched, so the clean version is always still there.

Batch it

If you are marking a set of photos, do them together so position, size and opacity match. A consistent set reads as deliberate; an inconsistent one reads as rushed.

The short version

Small, corner, 30–50% opacity, never on a face. And always keep the unmarked original.
