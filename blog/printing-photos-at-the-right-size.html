Printing photos at the right size

Print is unforgiving in a way screens are not. The numbers you need are simple, and so are the mistakes.

Sizing 26 May 2026 ·5 min read

A photo that looks perfect on a phone can print soft, cropped or oddly proportioned. Print has rules that screens let you ignore.

Start from the paper, not the file

Screens adapt to whatever you give them. Paper does not: it is a fixed physical size, and your photo has to fit it. So the first question is the print size, and the second is whether your photo has enough pixels for it.

The arithmetic is one multiplication: inches × 300 = pixels needed.

A modern phone photo at 12 MP prints beautifully up to A4. Beyond that you are relying on viewing distance — which, for a framed print on a wall, is usually fine.

The ratio trap

Phone photos are typically 4:3. Standard 6 × 4 prints are 3:2. These are not the same shape, so something gets cut.

If you let the print shop handle it, they crop from the centre and you may lose the top of someone's head. Crop to 3:2 yourself and you decide what goes.

Worth memorising: 6 × 4 and 12 × 8 are 3:2. 7 × 5 is 7:5. 10 × 8 is 5:4. A4 and A3 are roughly 1.41:1. Every one of them is a different shape from your camera's default.

Do not resize down for print

This is the opposite of everything else on this site. For screens, smaller is kind. For print, every pixel counts, so send the largest version you have and let the printer downsample if it needs to.

The only exception is an upload limit at the print service — and there, compress gently rather than reducing dimensions.

Borders, bleed and the trimmed edge

Printers trim slightly inside the paper edge, so a few millimetres around the outside may be lost. Do not put anything important right at the edge of the frame — dates, signatures, the top of a head.

Check before you order

Open the file at 100% on the biggest screen you have. If it looks soft there, it will look softer on paper. Most print services also show a warning when resolution is too low for the size ordered — it is worth taking seriously rather than clicking past.

The short version

Inches times 300 is the pixels you need. Crop to the paper's ratio yourself, and never resize down for print.
