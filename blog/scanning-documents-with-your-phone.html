Scanning documents with a phone camera, properly

A photograph of a page and a usable scan are different things. The gap is mostly light and framing.

Workflow 5 May 2026 ·5 min read

You do not need a scanner. You do need to stop photographing documents the way you photograph dinner.

Light is the whole game

Almost every unreadable document photo is a lighting failure. Three rules:

  • Use indirect daylight. Near a window, not in direct sun. Direct sun blows out white paper and creates hard shadows.
  • Never use the flash. It produces a bright hotspot in the middle and dark corners, and glares badly on glossy paper.
  • Watch your own shadow. Leaning over a page puts your head between the light and the paper. Stand to the side, or lift the phone higher and move yourself out of the way.

Shoot flat and square

Hold the phone parallel to the page, directly above the centre. A document shot at an angle has converging edges — a trapezoid rather than a rectangle — which looks unprofessional and makes text harder to read along the far edge.

Put the page on a dark, plain surface. The contrast makes the edges obvious, and gives you a clean line to crop to.

Crop hard

Crop to the page edges and nothing more. No table, no fingers, no floor. This is the single step that most improves both appearance and file size — you are often discarding half the frame, which halves the bytes before any compression.

Resolution: enough, and not more

Text needs more resolution than photographs to stay crisp, but not as much as you might think. For A4, around 1500–2000 px on the long edge keeps normal body text clearly legible. Above that you are mostly adding file size.

The test is simple: open it at 100% and read the smallest text on the page. If you can, it is enough.

Format: it depends on the page

A page of printed text with no photographs often does better as PNG, which keeps the edges of letters perfectly sharp. A page containing photographs or colour illustrations does better as JPG. If you are combining several pages into a PDF, JPG is usually the sensible default.

Combine into one PDF

Multi-page documents should arrive as one file, in order. Resize each page first, then combine — the PDF is only as small as the images inside it, and most portals cap uploads at 1 or 2 MB.

Read it before you send it

Open the finished file and actually read it. Every page present, right way up, in order, all four corners visible, every number legible. Thirty seconds of checking regularly saves a fortnight of waiting.

The short version

Indirect daylight, phone parallel to the page, crop to the edges, 1500–2000 px on the long edge. No flash, ever.
