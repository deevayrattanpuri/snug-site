Resume photos: the quiet rules nobody tells you

Whether to include one at all depends on where you are applying. If you do, the technical bar is higher than people assume.

Presets 19 May 2026 ·5 min read

Before the sizing question there is a bigger one: should there be a photo on your CV at all? The answer is regional, and getting it wrong is worse than any file size mistake.

Where photos are expected, and where they are a problem

In much of continental Europe, and in many Asian and Middle Eastern markets, a photo on a CV is conventional and its absence is noticed. In the UK, the US, Canada and Australia, the opposite is true: including one can cause an application to be set aside, because it exposes the employer to discrimination claims. Some applicant tracking systems reject documents with embedded images outright.

Check the norm for the country and the company before you decide. When genuinely unsure, leave it off a CV and rely on your professional profile picture instead.

If you are including one

The technical requirements are close to a passport photo, with slightly more latitude in framing:

  • Framing — head and shoulders, with the head occupying most of the frame
  • Ratio — usually portrait, around 3:4, or square for online profiles
  • Resolution — at least 300 DPI at the printed size, since CVs do get printed
  • Background — plain and uncluttered; a neutral wall is ideal
  • Expression — approachable. Unlike a passport photo, a slight smile is expected rather than forbidden
  • Clothing — what you would wear to the interview

Keep the document small

A high-resolution photo embedded in a Word document can push the file past an upload limit on its own. Resize the image before you place it: at the size it appears on the page, even 600–800 px on the long edge is usually ample, and the document stays comfortably under any cap.

The same photo, three shapes

One good sitting should give you everything: a portrait crop for the CV, a square crop for your professional profile, and a tighter crop for any application portal that asks for an ID-style image.

Shoot with a little space around your head so all three crops are available. Crop each one from the original rather than cropping a crop, so none of them is working from a degraded file.

Take it properly

  1. Daylight, from the front or slightly to one side. Never overhead, never direct flash.
  2. Rear camera, held by someone else, from about 1.5 to 2 metres. Selfie distance distorts faces.
  3. Camera at eye level — above flatters, below does not, and neither looks neutral.
  4. Plain background, with you standing far enough forward not to cast a shadow on it.
  5. Take twenty and pick one. Everyone looks wrong in most of them.

Name the file sensibly

firstname-surname-photo.jpg. It ends up in a recruiter's folder alongside hundreds of others, and IMG_20260519_144302.jpg helps nobody.

The short version

Check whether a photo belongs on a CV in that country first. If it does, shoot once and crop three versions from the original.
