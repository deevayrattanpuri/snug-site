Getting a profile picture right

Square file, circular display, tiny on screen. Three constraints that decide everything about the crop.

Presets 21 April 2026 ·4 min read

A profile picture is the most-viewed photo you own and the one people spend least time on. It is also displayed smaller than almost anything else, which changes what works.

Three constraints

It is square. Almost universally 1:1. Upload a rectangle and something crops it for you, usually from the centre.

It is shown as a circle. Most apps mask the square into a circle, so the corners are discarded. Anything near them is gone, and anything close to the edge is at risk.

It is small. In a chat list it may be 40 pixels across. Whatever you choose has to survive being the size of a fingernail.

What that means for the crop

  • Crop close. Head and shoulders. A full-body shot becomes an unidentifiable smudge at thumbnail size.
  • Centre the face, with margin all round so the circular mask takes background rather than an ear.
  • Favour contrast. A face against a plain, contrasting background reads at small sizes. A busy background does not.
  • Skip text. It is illegible at the size that matters.

Resolution

Something around 800 × 800 to 1200 × 1200 is plenty. It covers high-density screens and the larger display when someone taps through to your profile, without being wasteful.

Below about 400 px you risk softness on modern displays. Above about 1500 px you are uploading detail that will be downscaled and recompressed immediately.

Expect recompression

Every platform re-encodes profile pictures aggressively, because they serve them constantly at many sizes. Upload a clean, good-quality file and let the platform do its compression once. Uploading something you have already compressed hard means two rounds of damage, and that is where the blockiness comes from.

The squint test

Before you commit: shrink the image on your screen until it is roughly the size of a thumbnail, and look at it. Can you tell who it is? If not, crop closer and try again.

One sitting, several crops

The same photograph can give you a tight square for messaging, a slightly wider one for a professional network, and a portrait crop for anything that wants one. Crop each from the original rather than re-cropping a crop, so every version starts from full quality.

The short version

Crop close, centre the face, leave margin for the circle, and check it at thumbnail size before you upload.
