Why visa photos get rejected

The size is the easy part. These are the reasons applications actually come back.

Presets 12 May 2026 ·6 min read

Visa photo requirements are stricter than passport requirements in some countries and looser in others, and the one thing they share is that rejection costs you weeks. It is worth over-preparing.

The technical failures

These are the ones you can fix before submitting, and they are the minority of rejections:

  • Wrong dimensions — the required physical size varies by country
  • Wrong head height — the chin-to-crown measurement within the frame
  • Too low resolution — soft when printed at the required size
  • Over-compressed — visible artefacts, often from repeated re-saving
  • File too large or too small — portals specify both ends

The content failures

These cause most rejections, and no resizing tool can detect any of them:

  • Background — must be plain and uniform, usually white or off-white. A textured wall, a visible corner where two walls meet, or a shadow cast behind you will all fail.
  • Shadows on the face — usually caused by overhead lighting or standing too close to the background.
  • Expression — neutral, mouth closed, eyes open and clearly visible.
  • Glasses — many countries now disallow them entirely. Where permitted, no glare, no tinting, no frames across the eyes.
  • Hair across the face — the full face from hairline to chin must be visible.
  • Head covering — generally only for religious reasons, with the full face visible.
  • Age of the photo — commonly within six months, and they do check against previous submissions.
  • Digital alteration — filters, beauty modes and skin smoothing are disqualifying. Many phone cameras apply these automatically, so turn them off.

The automatic beautification problem

This one catches people who did everything else right. Many phones smooth skin and enlarge eyes by default, especially on the front camera. The result is technically an altered photograph, and increasingly it is detected.

Turn off any beauty or portrait enhancement, and use the rear camera, which is usually less aggressive.

Getting it right

  1. Find the official requirements for the specific country and visa type. Not a blog, not a third-party service — the official page.
  2. Plain white wall, stand a good metre in front of it so you do not cast a shadow.
  3. Even, soft, front-facing light. Daylight through a window, not a flash.
  4. Rear camera, held by someone else, at eye level, 1.5 to 2 metres away.
  5. Neutral expression, eyes open, hair clear of the face, no glasses unless permitted.
  6. Crop to the required ratio with the head at the required height.
  7. Resize to the exact pixel dimensions, save as JPG, meet the file size range.

Keep everything

Save the original photograph as well as the cropped submission. If the application is queried, or a second country wants a different size from the same sitting, you can produce it without photographing yourself again.

The short version

Most rejections are background, shadows and automatic beauty filters — not file size. Read the official page, not a summary.
