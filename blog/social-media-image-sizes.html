Social media image sizes: the principles that outlive the numbers

Exact pixel specs change every year. The rules behind them have not changed in a decade.

Presets 7 July 2026 ·6 min read

Every platform publishes recommended image dimensions, and every platform quietly changes them. Any article listing exact numbers is wrong within a year. What does not change is the reasoning, and once you understand that you can work out the right size for anything.

Principle one: upload larger than displayed, within reason

Screens vary enormously in pixel density. A high-density phone display packs two or three physical pixels into every layout pixel, so an image sized for the layout looks soft on it.

Uploading at roughly twice the displayed size covers this. Going far beyond that is wasted — the platform will downscale it anyway, and a 6000 px upload is simply slower for everyone.

Principle two: match the aspect ratio exactly

Platforms crop to their own shape. If you do not choose the crop, an algorithm does, and it usually takes the centre. The commonly used shapes:

PlacementTypical ratioWhy
Profile picture1:1Displayed in a circle — keep the subject centred
Feed post1:1 or 4:5Taller fills more of a phone screen
Story / reel9:16Full-screen vertical
Cover / banner16:9 or widerHeavily cropped on mobile — keep detail central
Link preview1.91:1The long-standing social sharing standard

Principle three: the circle will eat your corners

Profile pictures are square files displayed as circles. Anything in the corners is gone, and anything near the edge is at risk. Centre the subject with comfortable margin.

Principle four: banners get cropped differently everywhere

A cover image is shown wide on a desktop and much narrower on a phone, cropped from the centre. Put nothing important near the left or right edges — especially not text or a logo.

Principle five: everything gets recompressed

Platforms re-encode every upload to control their own bandwidth. You cannot prevent it, but you can avoid making it worse: upload a clean, high-quality file rather than one you have already compressed heavily. Compressing an already-compressed image is where the visible damage appears.

This is also why text in images degrades so badly on social platforms. If text must be legible, make it larger and higher contrast than feels necessary.

How to check the current numbers

Go to the platform's own help pages rather than a blog post. They are the only source that updates when the design does. Then apply the principles: match the ratio, upload at around twice the display size, keep the subject central, and do not pre-compress.

Making several versions from one photo

One good photograph can serve as a square profile picture, a 4:5 feed post and a 9:16 story — if it was shot with a little space around the subject. Crop each version deliberately from the original rather than re-cropping a crop, so every version comes from full quality.

The short version

Match the ratio, upload at about twice the display size, keep the subject centred, and never pre-compress.
